What Are Cloudflare Nameservers?

Customer question

How are Cloudflare nameservers different from Google DNS or OpenDNS? Can I use Cloudflare nameservers while keeping my website hosted elsewhere?

Customer-facing response

Hello,

Yes, you can generally keep your website hosted with your existing hosting provider while using Cloudflare for authoritative DNS.

Cloudflare authoritative nameservers hold and answer DNS queries for the records configured for your domain. Those records can point to a website hosted by another provider.

Google Public DNS and OpenDNS are commonly used as recursive DNS resolvers. They look up DNS information on behalf of users. This is different from the role of authoritative nameservers assigned to your domain.

For a typical full Cloudflare setup, update the nameservers at your domain registrar to the exact nameservers assigned to your Cloudflare zone. Before switching, make sure the required DNS records are present, including website and email records that you need.

Your hosting provider does not have to change. If you want to keep DNS hosted elsewhere, check whether a supported partial or CNAME setup is available for your account.

Kind regards,
Cloudflare Support

Troubleshooting rationale

The key distinction is authoritative DNS versus recursive DNS resolution. Changing authoritative nameservers changes where a domain's DNS zone is managed; it does not automatically move the website to another hosting provider.

Tools used and why

  • Cloudflare Dashboard: Check assigned nameservers and DNS records.
  • Domain registrar: Verify nameserver delegation.
  • dig / nslookup: Check authoritative DNS responses.
